Across the Atlantic, it's a completely different market. Hydro Deficit & El Niño Watch Frame the Supply Side

Europe's storage sits at five-year lows entering injection season while Norway's hydrological deficit deepens to levels not seen since 2011. Behind both: a winter drought that starved reservoirs and the early stages of a potentially record-breaking El Niño forming in the Pacific.

Nordic hydro balance is the dominant structural risk. Norway's snow reserves have fallen to their lowest levels in two decades — southern Norway's snowpack is the weakest since 1996 — creating a ~25 TWh energy deficit, nearly a fifth of total 2025 hydro output. The hydrological balance bottomed in late February at its lowest since 2011 and has barely recovered. Exports to the UK and Germany have plunged ~50% and ~40% YTD respectively, and Nordic power prices have surged to 4x 2025 levels. With spring melt yielding thin inflows, this deficit will persist well into summer, reducing gas-to-power substitution capacity on the continent.

EU gas storage at 28.9% — lowest since 2021. Injection season started a week late as demand briefly returned above seasonal norms in early April. The EU Gas Coordination Group confirmed levels sit below the 5-year average but noted steady inflows since early April as a positive signal. The 90% winter fill mandate will require sustained high injection rates through summer — a challenge given elevated TTF (~€60/MWh) and LNG competition linked to Middle East tensions. Storage at 327 TWh represents a significant structural headwind versus recent years.

El Niño Watch: the next regime shift is already brewing. NOAA's CPC has issued an El Niño Watch with a 61% probability of emergence by May-July. Latest forecasts suggest a potential Super El Niño (Niño 3.4 > +2.0°C), with impacts peaking in Winter 2026/27. For European gas markets, El Niño winters historically correlate with milder-than-normal conditions across northwest Europe, though the signal is noisy. The NOAA advisory confirms ENSO-neutral conditions prevailing through April-June (80%) before the transition. Meanwhile, a residual polar vortex core continues to fragment in the lower atmosphere, allowing late-season cold shots — but the stratospheric vortex is dissolving, signaling the regime shift is irreversible. Wind generation across Scandinavia was at its weakest since 2013 in Jan-Feb, compounding the hydro shortfall. Seasonal wind picks up into Q2, but the double-hit of drought + low wind has left power markets scrambling for thermal generation.

The structural backdrop is supply-constrained, not demand-driven. Hydro scarcity and tight inventories matter more than mild temps right now.

TurkStream Holds, Hormuz Remains Closed — Europe's Gas Diplomacy on Edge

Russia's remaining pipeline flows to Europe via TurkStream continue at ~15 bcm/yr, primarily serving Hungary, Serbia, and Austria. In the Middle East, the Strait of Hormuz remains effectively closed to commercial LNG traffic, constraining Qatar's export capacity and keeping JKM-TTF spreads elevated.

Russia pipeline flows via TurkStream remain stable. Despite the broader Russia-Ukraine transit shutdown since 2022, TurkStream continues delivering ~15 bcm/yr of Russian gas to southeastern Europe, primarily serving Hungary, Serbia, and Austria. There have been no nomination changes reported in the past 24 hours, per GIE and ENTSOG data. These flows represent less than 3% of pre-war EU Russian imports, but remain strategically critical for the recipient countries. No new sanctions have targeted TurkStream specifically, though broader EU restrictions on Russian energy infrastructure continue to evolve.

Hormuz remains closed to commercial LNG traffic. Following the April 6 escalation, the Strait of Hormuz has been effectively closed to commercial tankers for 38 days. Approximately 20-25% of global LNG supply (roughly 70-85 Mt/yr) transits Hormuz, primarily from Qatar, the UAE, and Oman. QatarEnergy has maintained force majeure declarations on damaged Ras Laffan trains, with full restart not expected before August 2026 at the earliest. The strait remains open to Iranian naval vessels only, with commercial traffic diverted via the Cape of Good Hope, adding 12-15 days of sailing time and $2-3/MMBtu in freight costs.

EU Russian LNG ban timeline and loopholes. The EU's 13th package of sanctions, adopted March 2026, targets spot Russian LNG imports by end-2026. Long-term contracts (estimated 10-12 bcm/yr to Spain, Belgium, France, and Greece) remain exempt pending their 2030-2035 expirations. The ban covers ~9 bcm/yr of spot cargoes, primarily to Zeebrugge, Montoir, and Gate terminals. Legal loopholes exist, including transshipment at third-country ports (Turkey, Egypt) and contract assignment, which could undermine enforcement. The Council adopted the package unanimously, but implementation challenges remain.

Next 2-4 weeks: Key diplomatic and regulatory events. US-Iran ceasefire talks are scheduled for April 11 in Pakistan, with Vice President Vance leading the US delegation. The EU Energy Security Council meets April 14 in Brussels to discuss LNG diversification and storage coordination. April 16 marks the scheduled end of the Troll A maintenance window in Norway, a critical supply constraint removal. No major Russian pipeline renegotiations are scheduled before May.
